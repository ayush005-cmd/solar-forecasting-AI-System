Algorithm Deep Dive

Why each method was chosen — in plain language

SVM

Why Support Vector Machines?

Solar irradiance and power output have a complex, non-linear relationship affected by temperature, humidity, and cloud cover simultaneously. SVM with an RBF kernel maps inputs into a high-dimensional space where a linear separator (the "hyperplane") captures these non-linearities. The margin-maximization objective also makes SVMs naturally robust to noisy sensor readings and outlier days.

Bagging

Why Bagging Ensemble?

Individual decision trees over-fit to training data. Bagging trains 100 trees on different random bootstrap samples of the dataset, then averages their predictions. This dramatically reduces variance — the primary source of error in solar forecasting — while keeping bias low. The result is a stable model that generalises well to unseen weather conditions.

LDA

Why Linear Discriminant Analysis?

Raw sensor data contains 10+ correlated features (temperature, humidity, pressure, irradiance, UV index, etc.). LDA projects them onto a lower-dimensional space that maximises class separability — distinguishing high-output days from low-output days. This reduces noise, removes redundant features, and speeds up downstream model training without losing predictive information.

Clustering

Why Site Clustering?

India has dramatically different solar regimes: arid Rajasthan receives 850+ W/m² irradiance while coastal Kerala averages 350 W/m². Training a single global model ignores this geography. K-Means clustering (k=4) automatically discovers these site groups, allowing cluster-specific model calibration and more accurate region-level forecasting.

MAE

Mean Absolute Error

Average absolute difference between predicted and actual output in kW. Lower is better. Easy to interpret — a MAE of 1.93 means predictions are off by 1.93 kW on average.

RMSE

Root Mean Square Error

Penalises large errors more than MAE by squaring them before averaging. Good for catching occasional large prediction failures. Lower is better.

R²

Coefficient of Determination

Proportion of variance in actual output explained by the model. Ranges 0–1. R²=0.951 means the model explains 95.1% of variability in solar output. Higher is better.

Site Cluster Discovery

K-Means clustering (k=4) of solar sites by irradiance and temperature regime

K-Means Scatter Plot — Irradiance vs Temperature
Cluster 0

High Irradiance — Arid

Rajasthan, Jodhpur, Jaisalmer. Very high solar irradiance (850+ W/m²), high temperature (38°C+). Best sites for large-scale solar farms. Avg output ~48 kW.

Cluster 1

Moderate — Temperate

Punjab, Haryana, Gujarat. Balanced irradiance (~580 W/m²) and mild temperature (~22°C). Consistent year-round production. Avg output ~30 kW.

Cluster 2

Low — Coastal / Humid

Kerala, Tamil Nadu coast. Lower irradiance (~350 W/m²) due to cloud cover and humidity. Suitable for rooftop solar. Avg output ~15 kW.

Cluster 3

Variable — Monsoon

Andhra Pradesh, Odisha. Medium irradiance (~470 W/m²) with high seasonal variability. Models trained here need strong temporal features.
